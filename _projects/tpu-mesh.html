Most of my thesis work was on origami, but kirigami is nonetheless interesting. This project was a short exploration into generalising the design of TPU mesh structures, the kind you often find on sharing platforms like Thingiverse. Instead of drawing each one by hand, I wrote a small PyQt configurator that generates disc-like kirigami meshes from a handful of parameters (concentric dashed rings, a hub, a flange, an optional opening and pull handles), previews them layer by layer, and exports a printable STL.

Everything is set up on an angular logic: the hub is a filled contour, the mesh is a stack of dashed and solid rings, the flange is a thickened outer ring with the same dash pattern, and the handles are arcs between two anchors on the flange. The opening is an angular sector that removes a portion of the rings and the flange.

Mesh generation

To create the mesh, we first define a base contour c. It is piecewise: straight segments and circular arcs (a circle, a slot, or a rounded rectangle), parametrised by the arclength fraction t ∈ [0, 1). Equal steps in t correspond to equal lengths along the contour, on straights and arcs alike.

The concentric rings are indexed by their position i = 0 … N−1, with N the number of rings. Ring i lies between the contour scaled by i·w and the contour scaled by (i+1)·w, with w the ring width. The hub is the filled contour out to the first rings, and the flange is the last ring. Since the rings are scaled copies of the contour, and not offset curves, their thickness is uniform only for the circle. On the slot and the rounded rectangle it increases at the round ends and at the corners.

Dashed rings are cut into n segments, each made of a dash and a gap. The duty δ is the fraction of each segment that is kept, and it varies linearly from the inner to the outer rings. Consecutive dashed rings are offset by half a segment, which places the cuts in quincunx. These staggered cuts are what allow the mesh to deform.

Different thicknesses

The mesh is kept as thin as possible so that it deforms. The hub and the flange are thicker to give it structure. In the first prototypes the flange had full thickness, which prevented it from folding. The flange now keeps the dash pattern: it is thick on the dashes and thinned in the gaps, so it stays structural but can fold.

Opening

The opening is defined by an angle, a position and a range of rings. Every arc of the selected rings and of the flange that falls inside the sector is removed.

Design and implementation

The generative logic was defined by the author from hand-made prototypes: a contour scaled into concentric rings, dashed rings cut in quincunx, a flange that follows the dash pattern. It was then implemented as a parametric configurator (Python, PyQt5, trimesh) with Claude (Anthropic) and tested against printed meshes.
